## 1. MX — Magnachip Semiconductor

**Sector / industry:** Technology / semiconductors.

### Insider signal

CEO **Chae Lee** purchased a total of **100,000 common shares** in genuine open-market transactions:

- Sep. 23: 50,000 shares at $3.17
- Sep. 23: 30,000 shares at $3.1866
- Sep. 24: 20,000 shares at $3.2199

Combined spend: **$318,496**.  
Fresh weighted-average entry: **$3.18496**, rounded to **$3.185**.

The SEC Form 4 uses transaction code **P** and shows direct ownership. This is a meaningful CEO purchase rather than a grant, exercise, private placement, or automatic-plan transaction.

### Vehicle / percentage-move potential

MX is exactly the type of structure this screen is designed to prefer: roughly **$141M market cap**, **27.8M-share float**, beta about **1.69**, ATR about **$0.22 on a $3.30 prior close (~6.7%)**, and recent realized volatility well above a slow industrial stock. The stock gained about **16.4% on Sep. 21**, and Monday premarket it was around **$3.87, +17.3%**.

At $3.87 the stock is already approximately **21.5% above Lee's $3.185 weighted entry**, so the insider filing has clearly been noticed. This is simultaneously the strongest evidence that the signal is working and the biggest chase-risk warning.

### Volume / participation

Friday volume was roughly **4.45M shares vs ~904k average = 4.92x normal**. The latest three completed sessions averaged about **2.00M = 2.22x normal**. Finviz showed roughly **493k premarket shares** by 08:50 ET and relative volume around **10.9x** at that point.

Unlike a financing-driven volume spike, the immediate price response is strongly positive, so here the volume acts as genuine confirmation.

### Catalyst / why the CEO may be buying

Magnachip recently announced a strategic investment and expanded relationship with **Navitas Semiconductor** around next-generation silicon-carbide power semiconductor solutions. The strategic relationship follows a July partnership intended to accelerate SiC adoption in high-voltage and ultra-high-voltage power markets. Magnachip has also been emphasizing power products for AI servers, EV charging, industrial and automotive uses.

The CEO purchase itself is now an immediate attention catalyst: Finviz noted a roughly 20% after-hours reaction when the filing appeared Friday.

### Financing / dilution check

There **is** fresh dilution and it deserves a penalty. Magnachip agreed to issue **1,461,988 new shares to Navitas at $3.42 for $5M**, roughly 4% of the existing share count. However, this is a strategic investment rather than a discounted toxic financing, the issue price is **above the CEO's average open-market entry**, and the CEO purchases themselves were separate true open-market transactions.

### Counterarguments / risks

- Already extended roughly **21.5% above the insider basis** before the open.
- Fresh strategic share issuance adds dilution.
- Low absolute market cap means both upside and downside can be violent.
- The filing-related pop may have already harvested much of the easy move.

### Why this could pop today

**A meaningful CEO open-market purchase hit an already explosive semiconductor microcap, participation exploded to ~5x normal Friday, and the market is visibly reacting before the bell.** MX is today's clearest percentage-move vehicle, but it is also the name with the greatest danger of chasing the signal after it has already fired.

## 2. DMRA — Damora Therapeutics

**Sector / industry:** Biotechnology.

### Insider signal

**Jennifer Jarrett, Director, President and CEO**, bought **12,500 ordinary shares at a weighted $20.23**, spending **$252,875** on Sep. 25.

The Form 4 is unusually explicit: the shares were **purchased on the open market at prevailing market prices using personal funds**, across trades from $20.10 to $20.40. The 500,000 RSUs listed in her total holdings are prior compensation and are **not** part of the purchase signal.

This appears to be the important fresh open-market CEO signal rather than a recent multi-insider cluster.

### Vehicle / percentage-move potential

DMRA has about a **$1.36B market cap**, **48.1M-share float**, beta about **1.41**, **9.8% short float**, realized volatility around **7-8%**, and ATR near **$1.81 on a ~$20.66 close (~8.8%)**. Recent sessions include approximately **-8.8%, -7.1%, -12.8% and -9.9%** moves, so a 5-10% day is clearly structurally plausible.

Monday premarket was around **$22.00, +6.5%**, approximately **8.7% above Jarrett's $20.23 entry**.

### Volume / participation

Friday volume was approximately **1.43M vs ~670k average = 2.14x normal**. The latest three completed sessions averaged about **1.12M = 1.68x normal**. That is supportive participation. Premarket volume was still thin at the available Finviz timestamp, so I would not treat the gap alone as fully volume-confirmed yet.

### Catalyst / likely reason for the buy

Damora is developing mutant-CALR-targeted therapeutics. It initiated the **Phase 1/1b CLARITY-101 trial of DMR-001** in mutant-CALR essential thrombocythemia and myelofibrosis. The company is still early clinically, and the disclosed development timeline points to proof-of-concept readouts beginning later rather than an immediate binary data event today.

The purchase therefore looks more like CEO conviction after a sharp recent drawdown than trading ahead of a confirmed near-term readout. DMRA is down roughly **28% over the past month**.

### Financing / dilution check

This is the biggest negative. Damora raised roughly **$316M gross in a February public offering at $19/share** and has an active ATM. An August amendment showed approximately **$120M of ATM capacity remaining** after roughly $30M had already been sold under the program.

That overhang is meaningful, although it is substantially smaller relative to DMRA's market cap than the ATM overhang in ANIX, and the company has a strong cash position with essentially no balance-sheet debt. There is no evidence that Jarrett's purchase was financing-linked; the Form 4 specifically says it was open-market and funded personally.

### Counterarguments / risks

- Active ATM creates dilution risk.
- Clinical-stage biotech with no immediate confirmed readout today.
- Premarket gap is already nearly 9% above the CEO entry.
- Binary clinical-development risk remains large.

### Why this could pop today

**A sitting President/CEO used personal funds to buy ~$253k of a biotech that routinely moves 7-13%, after a ~28% monthly drawdown, while recent volume is already ~1.7-2.1x normal.** The active ATM keeps it below MX despite a very clean Form 4.

Monday premarket was approximately **$3.90, +7.1%**, only **1.8% above Love's $3.83 weighted entry**. That is a better entry-positioning relationship than MX or DMRA.

### Volume / participation

Friday volume was roughly **3.5M**, almost exactly **1.0x normal**. The latest three sessions averaged about **2.64M = 0.76x normal**. Premarket volume was around **12.7k** at the available timestamp.

This is therefore not a volume-confirmed breakout yet. The signal improves materially if regular-session participation expands.

### Catalysts / context

Annexon announced a Sep. 24 presentation of baseline characteristics from its **Phase 3 ARCHER II pivotal geographic-atrophy trial**. The company has also disclosed a **Fall 2026 update** from the ANX1502 proof-of-concept study in cold agglutinin disease. Its tanruprubart and vonaprument programs are moving toward later-stage regulatory/commercial milestones.

### Financing / dilution check

Annexon entered a credit facility with Oxford Finance for **up to $200M**, drawing $50M initially. The company explicitly described this as **non-dilutive capital**. It is debt and therefore not free money, but it is materially preferable for this screen to a fresh ATM/public-offering overhang. No fresh equity financing tied to Love's purchase was found.

### Counterarguments / risks

- Recent completed-session volume is below normal.
- High short interest can amplify downside as well as upside.
- Clinical/regulatory development remains binary.
- The latest Phase 3 presentation concerns baseline characteristics rather than efficacy data.

### Why this could pop today

**CEO open-market buying near the current price, biotech realized volatility, nearly 20% short float, a fresh Phase 3 attention catalyst and no fresh equity raise make ANNX one of today's cleaner asymmetric vehicles.** Volume is the missing confirmation variable.

## ANIX — Anixa Biosciences: fresh CEO + director cluster, but active ATM is too large to ignore

ANIX was the important late addition during the final refresh. CEO **Amit Kumar bought 7,000 shares at $2.78** and director **Arnold Baskies bought 5,000 at $2.76**, both filed Monday morning. Those two fresh purchases total **12,000 shares / $33,260 / weighted average $2.7717**.

A third director, **Lewis Titterton**, bought **49,036 shares at $2.69 for $131,907** on Sep. 23, but his Form 4 was filed Friday at **08:39 ET**, before today's eligibility window. It is therefore broader recent accumulation rather than a fresh-window filing. Combining all three gives **61,036 shares / ~$165.2k / weighted average ~$2.706**.

The vehicle itself is decent: roughly **$97M market cap**, **32.8M float**, realized volatility around **5.5-6.3%**, ATR around **6%**, and active ovarian CAR-T / breast-cancer-vaccine programs. Monday premarket was only about **$2.80, +0.7%**, roughly 1% above the fresh cluster entry.

The reason it is **not** on the actionable board is the financing check. Anixa's latest 10-Q says it sold **1.55M shares through its ATM during the nine months ended July 31 for ~$4.8M net**, and still had approximately **$95M of common-stock capacity available under the active ATM**. That remaining capacity is nearly the size of ANIX's current market capitalization. The company did have **no warrants outstanding at July 31**, which is positive, but the ATM overhang is too large to ignore under this strategy's dilution rule.

**Verdict:** excellent insider-pattern improvement, but the active ATM prevents me from promoting it above cleaner candidates unless price/volume action becomes unusually strong.

# Final ranking and decision notes

**MX > DMRA > ANNX > SKIL.**

- **MX** is the strongest pure pop signal and is already proving it premarket, but it is also the worst chase at ~21.5% above the CEO's cost basis.
- **DMRA** has the cleanest large personal-money CEO purchase plus strong recent participation, but its active ATM is a real dilution penalty.
- **ANNX** has excellent positioning close to the CEO entry, nearly 20% short float and cleaner financing, but volume is not yet confirming.
- **SKIL** has the most persistent recent accumulation and extreme microcap reflexivity, but current participation is too weak.
- **ANIX** would otherwise have made the board after the late CEO/director filings, but the active ~$95M ATM capacity is too large relative to its ~$97M market cap to wave away.

The final Finviz refresh at roughly **08:50 ET** showed ANIX's CEO and director filings as the newest material high-signal additions. A later DX CIO filing at 08:43 ET was reviewed and rejected by the vehicle gate. No filing after that displaced the four actionable names above.
