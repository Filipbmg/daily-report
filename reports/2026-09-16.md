## 1. NTHI — NeOnc Technologies Holdings

### Signal

This is the strongest buyer-quality setup in the overnight batch. Newly reported Form 4s show **Amir Heshmatpour, CEO and President**, buying 15,000 shares at $3.96 on Sep. 14 and another 35,000 at a $3.297 weighted average on Sep. 15. A separate Form 4 shows **Thomas C. Chen, CEO**, buying 5,556 shares at $3.60 and 5,242 at $3.81, while CFO Keithly Garnett added a small 257-share purchase around $3.89.

The key point is not the CFO's symbolic $1k. It is the **multiple top-level insiders buying around the same repricing zone**, led by Heshmatpour putting roughly $175k to work personally. SEC Form 4s mark the transactions with code **P**, confirming genuine purchases.

Across the newly reported cluster, the purchases total roughly **61,055 shares and $215.8k**, with a weighted entry around **$3.53**.

### Catalyst and price context

NTHI is an approximately $82m clinical-stage neuro-oncology company. The company recently reported positive Phase 2a NEO100 data in recurrent IDH1-mutant high-grade glioma, with six-month progression-free survival of **48.9% versus a pre-specified 20% benchmark**, and plans to seek an FDA Type B meeting regarding a registrational path.

The complication is financing. On Sep. 9 NeOnc announced a **$15m registered direct offering** involving 3.57m shares or pre-funded warrants plus warrants at $4.20. The stock subsequently repriced sharply and closed Sep. 15 at **$3.31, down 12.4% on the day**, versus $5.03 on Sep. 4.

That makes the insider behavior unusually informative: senior insiders are buying into a post-financing/post-data drawdown rather than after an easy rally.

### Volume

Sep. 15 volume was **285.8k** against a 20-day average around **201.8k**, roughly **1.42x normal**. The Sep. 11-15 three-session average was about **255k, or 1.26x normal**.

Interpretation: this is not clean bullish volume because Tuesday's elevated participation accompanied a 12% decline. But the volume proves the repricing is liquid enough to matter. If the insider cluster flips price green on similarly elevated volume, that would be strong reversal confirmation.

### Why this could pop today

**Real C-suite cluster + tiny biotech market cap + recent positive clinical data + fresh financing overhang already partially repriced + insiders buying around current levels.** This is exactly the kind of combination that can attract fast-money attention when the Form 4s hit screens.

### Risks

The offering created dilution and warrants, the stock is still in a downtrend, and short interest is not extreme enough to guarantee squeeze mechanics. Today's Fed event also raises small-cap volatility.

**Verdict: clear #1.**

## 2. TENX — Tenax Therapeutics

### Signal

TENX has a genuine **CEO + CFO cluster**. CEO Christopher Giordano bought **6,000 shares at $1.875** on Sep. 11 and another **4,850 at $1.8221** on Sep. 15. CFO Thomas Staab II bought **5,675 shares at about $1.935**. SEC filings show transaction code **P**.

Combined, the two executives bought roughly **16,525 shares for $31.1k**, weighted around **$1.88**. Dollar size is modest, but buyer quality is excellent for this screen.

### Why they may be buying

TENX was destroyed after its Phase 3 LEVEL study missed its primary endpoint, falling roughly 85% in the original shock. The important nuance is that subsequent analyses showed a signal in sicker patients, including a **26.3-meter improvement versus placebo (p=0.0112)** in patients below the median baseline six-minute walk distance. Management is arguing that this may support a narrower development/regulatory path.

So this looks like classic **post-binary-event insider conviction**: management is buying after the market largely wrote off the program.

### Volume

Sep. 15 volume was about **1.63m** versus a 20-day average around **7.22m**, only **0.23x normal**. The latest three sessions averaged about **2.10m, or 0.29x normal**.

That is a warning. Participation has faded dramatically after the clinical-event shock. The positive interpretation is that forced selling may be exhausting; the negative interpretation is that buyers have not returned yet.

### Why this could pop today

TENX has **CEO+CFO buying, a ~$105m market cap, ~30% short float, an extremely depressed chart, and an active debate over whether the failed Phase 3 contains a salvageable subgroup signal**. That is highly convex if volume returns.

### Risks

The primary endpoint failure is real, analyst downgrades followed, and management's subgroup thesis still faces clinical and regulatory uncertainty. Low recent volume means the insider filing may not immediately attract enough participation.

**Verdict: #2, but requires volume confirmation.**
